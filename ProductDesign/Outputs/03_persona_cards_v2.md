## Budget Guardian + financial decision defender

**Demographics**

- Role: controller, finance lead, or asset manager with budget accountability
- Company size: mid-size to large organization
- Portfolio scope: single site to multi-building portfolio
- Market type: deregulated markets with recurring renewal decisions

**Primary goals**

- Forecast energy costs accurately enough to defend a budget
  Sources: Cheri B ("generating budgeting costs is a huge thing"), Nick D Cushman ("you don't want to go over budget... it just comes off like you're not planning properly," lines 263–271), Chip Walker ("take last year's and increase by 5 to 8%," line 495), Gary Graham ("year to year over year predictability delivery on that budget is extremely important for the property manager," line 180), Gil Kaufman Jacobs
- Prove whether a proposed rate is actually better than the status quo
  Sources: Gil Kaufman Jacobs ("Here's what you would pay if you stick with your utility rate and here's how we can save you," line 1143), Thomas H ("how much am I saving by going through Aquarius"), Otha Smith ("it would be awesome if it could just drive like a bottom line estimated savings," line 47), Andy Bokalders ("what am I comparing against?," line 46)
- Avoid surprises at renewal time
  Sources: Chris C ("very important; we can't let the contract expire"), Ryan D (renewal alerts = "most important feature"), Gary Graham ("property manager doesn't want surprises," line 176), Nick D Cushman (budget variance concern, lines 263–283)

**Top pain points with source quotes**

- Savings claims are hard to trust without a baseline.
  "Here's what you would pay if you stick with your utility rate and here's how we can save you."
  Source: Gil Kaufman Jacobs interview, line 1143
  Corroborated by: Otha Smith ("it would be awesome if it could just drive like a bottom line estimated savings," line 47), Andy Bokalders ("We're leaving it all to them to figure that out," line 60), Thomas H ("how much am I saving by going through Aquarius" — synthesis Finding 1)
- Brokers promise savings without proving them.
  "you're going to save, you know, 10, 15% with us, and then there's no effort to back that up"
  Source: Gil Kaufman Jacobs interview, line 1155
  Corroborated by: Thomas H ("how much am I saving by going through Aquarius" — wants proof, not claims), Andy Bokalders (describes "price check" behavior — customers using portal to verify broker, line 46–50), Gary Graham ("predictability delivery on that budget is extremely important," line 180 — PM cares about proof over promises)
- The ideal output is a simple savings summary, not another spreadsheet.
  "summaries usually help us best... if my e-mail goes past four sentences, I know the person's not reading it anymore."
  Source: Nick D Cushman interview, lines 1091–1107
  Corroborated by: Gil Kaufman Jacobs ("Just tell me what we saved and what percentage that is, and I'm happy with that," line 1219), Andy Bokalders ("I didn't sign up to read a novel," line 94), Michael G/GMS ("I am a big fan of the bullet points" — synthesis)

**Decision criteria**

- Clear comparison to current rate or default utility service
  Sources: Gil Kaufman Jacobs (line 1143), Otha Smith (line 47), Andy Bokalders (line 46), Thomas H, Cheri B, Chris C — synthesis Finding 1 (8+ sources)
- Shareable financial summary for leadership review
  Sources: Nick D Cushman ("Lauren gave me a summary... I just presented it to ownership," line 171), Chris C (needs export for committee), Marilyn M (export capability), Michael G/Barclays (needs PDF for committee)
- Confidence that the portal reflects real savings, not marketing claims
  Sources: Gil Kaufman Jacobs (line 1155), Thomas H ("how much am I saving"), Andy Bokalders (price-check behavior, line 46–50), Cheri B ("everyone says" no hidden fees — synthesis Warlock section)

**Representative quote**

"summaries usually help us best... if my e-mail goes past four sentences, I know the person's not reading it anymore."
Source: Nick D Cushman interview, lines 1091–1107

*Gil's original quote ("Just tell me what we saved and what percentage that is") remains the strongest single articulation of the savings summary need, but Nick's quote better captures the cross-cutting brevity theme supported by Andy ("read a novel"), Michael/GMS ("bullet points"), and Thomas ("grid format").*

**Design implications**

- Show current spend or utility baseline beside every recommended option
- Default to annualized savings language, not raw cents-per-kWh only
- Make exports brief enough for budget and owner review

## Portfolio Operator + multi-site workload minimizer

**Demographics**

- Role: property manager, asset manager, or operations lead
- Company size: small to mid-size firm managing many properties
- Portfolio scope: multi-location portfolio with staggered contracts and meters
- Market type: mixed regional portfolio with ongoing renewals

**Primary goals**

- Keep contracts and meter details organized across locations
  Sources: Marilyn M (1,700+ stores, "spreadsheets and Google alerts"), Gil Kaufman Jacobs ("spreadsheet with all the meters," line 995), Michael G/GMS (50 locations, 8 deregulated states), Nick D Cushman (4 buildings with different owners, line 419)
- Renew on time without chasing spreadsheets
  Sources: Chris C ("very important; we can't let the contract expire"), Ryan D (renewal alerts = "most important feature"), Trey Smoyer (TX shutoff risk, manual renewal notification workaround, lines 251–257), Gil Kaufman Jacobs (line 1575)
- Present a simple recommendation to ownership
  Sources: Nick D Cushman ("Lauren gave me a summary... I just presented it to ownership," line 171), Gary Graham ("reporting up to the asset manager... budget predictability is extremely important," lines 176–180), Gil Kaufman Jacobs (needs defensible data — synthesis persona archetype sources)

**Top pain points with source quotes**

- Portfolio operations still live in spreadsheets.
  "I have a spreadsheet with all the meters dedicated to all the different spaces at all the different buildings."
  Source: Gil Kaufman Jacobs interview, line 995
  Corroborated by: Marilyn M ("spreadsheets and Google alerts — it's not sophisticated" — synthesis Finding 10), Nick D Cushman ("she gave me a list of vendors... put it in a spreadsheet," line 627), Trey Smoyer (exports from admin, manually formats in Excel, screenshots — synthesis Finding 10), Chip Walker (manual budgeting: "take last year's and increase by 5 to 8%," line 495)
- Spreadsheet-based reminders are fragile.
  "It's like, I'll remind myself to follow up the utility company to do that in a spreadsheet, which is not a super effective way to do that"
  Source: Gil Kaufman Jacobs interview, line 1575
  Corroborated by: Trey Smoyer (manual renewal notification process — exports, screenshots, emails "for EVERY customer, on a monthly basis," lines 251–257), Andy Bokalders ("He found my old email from when I helped him out in August" — customer relying on email memory for follow-up, line 70), Marilyn M (wants dashboard for purchased contracts — synthesis)
- Nothing warns them before something falls through the cracks.
  "Nothing falls through the cracks, which it easily can when you're kind of just doing this based on an Excel spreadsheet that doesn't notify me or anything."
  Source: Gil Kaufman Jacobs interview, line 1599
  Corroborated by: Chris C ("very important; we can't let the contract expire" — synthesis Finding 6), Ryan D (renewal alerts = "most important feature" — synthesis Finding 6), Trey Smoyer (traffic light concept: expired contracts in TX can result in shutoff; "why didn't you tell me?" is a real complaint — synthesis Finding 6)

**Decision criteria**

- One place to see locations, contracts, and renewal timing
  Sources: Marilyn M (dashboard for purchased contracts), Michael G/GMS (50 locations needing consolidated view), Gil Kaufman Jacobs (line 995), Trey Smoyer (expiration dates with hyperlinks, line 57), Nick D Cushman (4 buildings with staggered dates)
- Alerts before action is needed
  Sources: Chris C, Ryan D, Trey Smoyer, Cheri B, Tsui Y, Michael G/GMS — synthesis Finding 6 (7+ sources)
- A summary simple enough to hand to ownership
  Sources: Nick D Cushman (line 171), Gary Graham (lines 176–180), Michael G/GMS ("bullet points"), Thomas H ("grid format")

**Representative quote**

"Lauren gave me a summary of the different prices and bids, and I just presented it to ownership."
Source: Nick D Cushman interview, line 171

**Design implications**

- Organize the dashboard around portfolios and upcoming actions, not just locations
- Give every contract a clear status and alert window
- Support exportable summaries for owner approval

## Skeptical Evaluator + trust-first relationship buyer

**Demographics**

- Role: property leader or senior manager influencing purchase decisions
- Company size: lean team with many competing priorities
- Portfolio scope: periodic procurement review rather than daily portal use
- Market type: relationship-driven buying motion with owner oversight

**Primary goals**

- Validate that recommendations are credible before acting
  Sources: Elena V ("it's not just about the rates... also about the supplier and the long-term relationship"), Gil Kaufman Jacobs ("The challenge for me is always, okay, you guys are going to save us 20%. How do you really prove that to me?" — synthesis), Andy Bokalders (describes "price check" behavior — customers use portal to verify broker, lines 46–50), Kaljit usability test ("I don't know why this algorithm is putting this one as the best value")
- Keep a human backstop in the decision process
  Sources: Nick D Cushman ("the human interaction and discussion aspect is always more favorable," line 687), Elena V ("I personally prefer to talk to a person" — synthesis), Gary Graham (describes property manager quarterly meetings and relationship dynamics, line 271)
- Get concise information instead of dense screens or long emails
  Sources: Nick D Cushman ("if my e-mail goes past four sentences, I know the person's not reading it anymore," line 1091), Andy Bokalders ("I didn't sign up to read a novel," line 94), Michael G/GMS ("bullet points"), Thomas H ("grid format")

**Top pain points with source quotes**

- Portal-only self-service is less persuasive than a live discussion.
  "the human interaction and discussion aspect is always more favorable, in my opinion"
  Source: Nick D Cushman interview, line 687
  Corroborated by: Elena V ("I personally prefer to talk to a person" — synthesis), Gary Graham (property managers rely on relationship and quarterly meetings with advisors, line 271), Gil Kaufman Jacobs (prefers talking to people, uses portal for data — synthesis persona archetype)
- Dense content loses attention quickly.
  "I know if my e-mail goes past four sentences, I know the person's not reading it anymore."
  Source: Nick D Cushman interview, line 1091
  Corroborated by: Andy Bokalders ("I didn't sign up to read a novel. When I think of new technology, it's pictures and graphs and charts," line 94), Michael G/GMS ("I am a big fan of the bullet points" — synthesis), Thomas H ("grid format" — synthesis)
- They still need a simple owner-facing package.
  "We look into it and we can present it to ownership."
  Source: Nick D Cushman interview, line 151
  Corroborated by: Nick D Cushman ("Lauren gave me a summary of the different prices and bids, and I just presented it to ownership," line 171), Gary Graham ("they're reporting up to the asset manager... budget predictability... extremely important for the property manager," lines 176–180), Gil Kaufman Jacobs (needs defensible data for leadership — synthesis persona archetype)

**Decision criteria**

- Clear explanation of why a plan is recommended
  Sources: Kaljit usability test ("I don't know why this algorithm is putting this one as best value"), Nick Evans ("they want more — they want to understand why" — synthesis Finding 13), Andy Bokalders ("Legal doesn't like the word 'best'" — synthesis Finding 13)
- Access to a person when context or trust is missing
  Sources: Nick D Cushman (line 687), Elena V (synthesis), Trey Smoyer ("schedule a call" button request — synthesis single-source findings)
- Short, scannable summaries for owner review
  Sources: Nick D Cushman (lines 1091, 1107), Michael G/GMS ("bullet points"), Andy Bokalders (line 94), Thomas H ("grid format")

**Representative quote**

"the human interaction and discussion aspect is always more favorable, in my opinion"
Source: Nick D Cushman interview, line 687

**Design implications**

- Keep recommended-plan rationale visible and plain-language
- Surface support paths on every decision screen
- Use concise summaries and progressive disclosure instead of long text blocks
